The 50/30/20 Budget: A Simple Plan for Any Paycheck

How to split take-home pay into needs, wants and savings, adapt the rule to high-cost cities and lower incomes, and build an emergency fund that actually gets finished.

By the 34456.com Editorial Team · Fact-checked against IRS and CRA publications ·

The rule in one line

Spend about 50% of take-home pay on needs, 30% on wants and send 20% to savings and extra debt payments. It's simple enough to stick to and flexible enough to adjust.

Worked example on $34,456

A single US filer earning $34,456 with no state income tax takes home about $2,489 a month. The 50/30/20 split gives roughly $1,244 for needs, $747 for wants and $498 for savings. At $498 a month, a three-month emergency fund of needs ($3,733) takes about 7.5 months to build; six months takes about 15.

When needs exceed 50%

In expensive cities rent alone can pass 40% of take-home pay. Rather than abandoning the plan, protect the savings slice first (even 10% builds momentum), trim wants, and attack the biggest fixed costs: housing, transport and insurance. Roommates, refinancing and shopping insurance annually move the needle more than skipping coffee.

Automate it

  1. Have savings leave your account on payday, automatically.
  2. Put needs on a dedicated account with bills on autopay.
  3. Whatever is left in the spending account is guilt-free wants money.

Level up with every raise

Each time your pay rises, send at least half of the increase to savings before you get used to it. Lifestyle grows slower, savings grow faster, and you don't feel a cut.

Frequently asked questions

Does 20% include retirement contributions taken from my paycheck?

You can count them. If 6% already goes to a 401(k) pre-tax, aim to save another 10–14% from take-home pay.

Where should an emergency fund live?

In a separate, insured, high-yield savings account you can access within a day or two.
