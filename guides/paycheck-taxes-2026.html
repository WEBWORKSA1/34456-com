Where Your Paycheck Goes in 2026: Taxes, FICA and Deductions Explained

A plain-English walk through 2026 federal tax brackets, the standard deduction, Social Security and Medicare — plus the Canadian equivalents and legal ways to keep more.

By the 34456.com Editorial Team · Fact-checked against IRS and CRA publications ·

Step 1: Gross pay minus pre-tax deductions

Your paycheck starts with gross pay. Contributions to a traditional 401(k), 403(b), HSA or employer health premiums usually come out before income tax, shrinking the income the IRS taxes. In Canada, RRSP contributions work similarly when you file.

Step 2: The standard deduction

For tax year 2026 the IRS standard deduction is $16,100 for single filers, $32,200 for married couples filing jointly and $24,150 for heads of household. The first slice of income up to that amount is effectively tax-free.

Step 3: Progressive brackets

Seven federal rates apply to taxable income: 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35% and 37%. For a single filer in 2026, 10% applies up to $12,400, 12% up to $50,400 and 22% up to $105,700; the 37% rate starts above $640,600. Each rate taxes only the income inside its band, so moving into a higher bracket never reduces take-home pay.

Worked example — $34,456, single: taxable income = $34,456 − $16,100 = $18,356. Tax = 10% × $12,400 + 12% × $5,956 = $1,240 + $714.72 = $1,954.72.

Step 4: FICA

Social Security takes 6.2% of wages up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500. Medicare takes 1.45% of all wages, plus an extra 0.9% above $200,000 ($250,000 for joint filers). On $34,456 that's $2,136.27 and $499.61.

Step 5: State and local tax

Nine states have no tax on wages, while others range from low flat rates to progressive systems topping out above 10%. Some cities add their own income tax.

Canada in brief

For 2026 the lowest federal rate is 14% on income up to $58,523, with the basic personal amount at $16,452. Employees also contribute to the Canada Pension Plan (5.95% between $3,500 and $74,600, plus CPP2 above that) and Employment Insurance (1.63% up to $68,900 of insurable earnings). Provinces add their own brackets.

Five legal ways to keep more

  1. Contribute enough to get your full employer match.
  2. Use an HSA if you have a qualifying health plan.
  3. Review your W-4 or TD1 after marriage, a new child or a second job.
  4. Claim credits you qualify for — they reduce tax dollar-for-dollar.
  5. Time bonuses and side income with a calculator before they arrive.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 2026 standard deduction?

$16,100 single, $32,200 married filing jointly and $24,150 head of household.

What is the 2026 Social Security wage base?

$184,500. Wages above that are not subject to the 6.2% Social Security tax.
