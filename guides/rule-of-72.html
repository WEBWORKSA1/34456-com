The Rule of 72: Double Your Money (or Your Debt) in Your Head

Use one division to estimate how fast savings double — and how fast high-interest debt does too. Includes a table, examples and the math behind it.

By the 34456.com Editorial Team · Fact-checked against IRS and CRA publications ·

The rule

Divide 72 by an annual interest rate to estimate how many years it takes money to double. At 6%, about 12 years. At 8%, about 9. At 12%, about 6.

RateRule of 72Exact (annual compounding)
3%24.0 yrs23.4 yrs
5%14.4 yrs14.2 yrs
7%10.3 yrs10.2 yrs
10%7.2 yrs7.3 yrs
24%3.0 yrs3.2 yrs

Why 72?

The exact doubling time is ln(2) ÷ ln(1 + r). For everyday rates that's close to 0.693 ÷ r, and 72 is a handy nearby number with many divisors (2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 12), which makes mental math easy.

It works against you too

A credit card at 24% APR doubles an unpaid balance in about three years. That's why paying off high-interest debt is often the best “investment” available — a guaranteed return equal to the interest rate.

Inflation version

At 3% inflation, prices double in about 24 years. Money in a 0.5% account loses roughly half its purchasing power over that time.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Rule of 72 exact?

No, it is an approximation that is most accurate between about 6% and 10%.

Can I use it for monthly compounding?

Yes, it remains a close estimate; monthly compounding doubles money slightly faster.
