What this calculator includes

United States (tax year 2026): the IRS standard deduction ($16,100 single, $32,200 married filing jointly, $24,150 head of household), the seven federal brackets from 10% to 37%, Social Security at 6.2% up to the $184,500 wage base, Medicare at 1.45% plus the 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax above $200,000 ($250,000 joint), and a flat state/local rate you choose.

Canada (2026): the five federal brackets starting at 14% up to $58,523, the $16,452 basic personal amount and Canada employment amount credits, CPP at 5.95% between $3,500 and $74,600 plus CPP2 at 4% up to $85,000, and EI at 1.63% on insurable earnings up to $68,900. Quebec residents pay QPP and a lower EI rate and receive a federal abatement, so treat Quebec results as approximate.

Marginal vs. effective rate

Your marginal rate is the tax on your next dollar. Your effective rate is total tax divided by gross pay. Because brackets only tax the slice of income inside them, a raise never lowers your take-home pay.

Three legal ways to raise take-home pay

  1. Contribute pre-tax to a 401(k), 403(b), HSA or RRSP — each dollar lowers taxable income.
  2. Update your W-4 (US) or TD1 (Canada) after life changes so you are not over-withheld.
  3. Claim every credit you qualify for — child, education and saver's credits can be worth thousands.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my paycheck smaller than gross ÷ pay periods?

Income tax, Social Security and Medicare (or CPP and EI in Canada), state or provincial tax and any benefit deductions are withheld before you are paid.

Does this replace a payroll calculator?

No. It is an annualised estimate. Real paychecks depend on your W-4/TD1, benefits, local taxes and timing of bonuses.

What tax year is used?

The 2026 IRS and CRA amounts. We update the tables every year after the IRS and CRA publish inflation adjustments.
