The loan payment formula

Payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n)
P = principal, r = monthly rate (annual ÷ 12), n = number of monthly payments

On a $300,000 loan at 6.5% for 30 years the principal-and-interest payment is about $1,896 and total interest is roughly $382,600 — more than the original loan. Adding just $200 a month cuts years off the term and saves well over $80,000 in interest. Mortgages also carry property tax, insurance and possibly PMI or HOA fees, so budget for the full housing payment.

Affordability rule of thumb

Many lenders look for housing costs under about 28% of gross monthly income and total debt payments under about 36%. On a $34,456 salary ($2,871/month), that suggests housing near $804 and all debts under $1,034 a month.

Frequently asked questions

Does paying extra really help?

Yes. Extra payments go straight to principal, so every later payment carries less interest. The earlier you pay extra, the bigger the saving.

Should I choose 15 or 30 years?

A 15-year loan has higher payments but far lower total interest and usually a lower rate. A 30-year loan keeps payments flexible; you can still pay extra.
