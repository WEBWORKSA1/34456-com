The anatomy of a raise request that works

  1. Evidence first. Lead with measurable results — revenue, time saved, customers retained.
  2. Anchor with a number. Name a specific figure backed by market data, slightly above what you'd accept.
  3. Ask for a meeting, not a decision. Email sets the agenda; the conversation closes it.
  4. Have a plan B. If budget is frozen, negotiate title, PTO, remote days, training budget or a dated review.
  5. Follow up in writing. Confirm any agreement by email the same day.

Frequently asked questions

Is it risky to ask for a raise?

Asking professionally, with evidence, is normal and expected in most workplaces. The bigger risk is the compounding cost of never asking.

How much should I ask for?

Commonly 5–10% for a raise in role and 10–20% when changing jobs, anchored to market data for your role and location.
