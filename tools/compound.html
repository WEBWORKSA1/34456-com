Why compounding is the most powerful number in personal finance

Compound interest earns interest on previous interest. Early on, contributions do most of the work; later, growth overtakes them. Saving $300 a month for 25 years at 7% turns $90,000 of contributions (plus a $5,000 start) into roughly $270,000.

The Rule of 72

Divide 72 by your annual rate to estimate how many years it takes money to double: about 10.3 years at 7%, 7.2 years at 10%. It works just as powerfully against you on debt — at 24% APR a credit-card balance doubles in about three years.

Frequently asked questions

What rate should I use?

Use a conservative estimate. High-yield savings accounts pay a few percent; diversified stock portfolios have historically returned more but with large swings. Test a range.

Does compounding frequency matter much?

A little. Moving from annual to monthly compounding at 7% raises the effective yield from 7.00% to about 7.23%. Time and contribution size matter far more.
